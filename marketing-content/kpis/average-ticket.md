# Average Ticket

**URL:** `/kpis/average-ticket`
**Meta title:** Average Ticket: What It Is and How to Move It | The Good Plans Co
**Meta description:** Average ticket is the average dollar value of each transaction. The number that tells you whether your pricing and service mix are working.

---

## Definition

Average ticket is the average dollar value per transaction, job, or sale in your business. It tells you, on average, how much each customer is worth when they buy.

## Formula

```
Average Ticket = Total Revenue ÷ Number of Transactions
```

You can run this over any time window. Weekly, monthly, or year-to-date. Different windows answer different questions.

## What it tells you

Average ticket is one of three numbers that produces revenue:

```
Revenue = Number of Transactions × Average Ticket × Close Rate
```

If you want more revenue, you have three levers: more transactions, higher average ticket, or better close rate. Most owners only think about transaction volume. Average ticket is often the easier lever and is almost always under-pulled.

A rising average ticket means you're either pricing better, selling larger bundles, or shifting toward higher-value services. A falling average ticket means the opposite is happening, often without anyone noticing.

## Common mistakes

- **Looking at it in aggregate only.** A blended average ticket across all services can hide a service line where average is sliding. Break it out by category.
- **Not tracking it over time.** A single average ticket number doesn't tell you much. The TREND tells you everything.
- **Confusing higher average ticket with better business.** A business that doubles its average ticket but loses half its customers may be worse off, depending on close rate and labor cost. Look at it in context of the revenue equation.

## How to improve it

Three reliable levers:

**1. Review pricing.**
The simplest. If you haven't touched your pricing in 18+ months, raising it is almost certainly the lowest-effort revenue move available. Most service businesses can take a 5-10% price increase without losing meaningful business.

**2. Sell bundles or larger packages.**
Make the larger option easy to choose. If customers default to your smallest offering, your average ticket reflects that. Restructure your offerings so the middle or larger option is the most natural pick.

**3. Train the close.**
If your team isn't presenting the larger options confidently, customers default to the smaller ones. This is often a service-advisor or salesperson training issue, not a pricing issue. Look at average ticket by employee — if there's variance, training is the lever.

## What "good" looks like

Healthy average ticket varies wildly by business type. There's no universal benchmark.

The more useful test: is your average ticket trending up, flat, or down? Trending up means you're moving in the right direction. Flat for 12+ months means you're falling behind inflation (your real average ticket is declining). Trending down is a red flag — somewhere in the system, something is eroding the value per transaction.
