# Labor Efficiency

**URL:** `/kpis/labor-efficiency`
**Meta title:** Labor Efficiency: Definition, Formula, and How to Improve It | The Good Plans Co
**Meta description:** Labor efficiency is how productively your team's hours are being used. Different from labor utilization. Here's what it tells you and how to move it.

---

## Definition

Labor efficiency is the ratio of productive output hours to actual hours worked by your team. It tells you how productively the time you're paying for is being used.

## Formula

```
Labor Efficiency % = Productive Hours ÷ Total Hours Worked × 100
```

"Productive Hours" varies by business type:

- **Service businesses:** billable hours, or hours delivering paid work
- **Trade businesses:** hours on the job, not in transit or waiting
- **Production businesses:** hours producing output, not setup or downtime

The hours-worked denominator should include everything you pay for: actual work, breaks, waiting, setup, travel between jobs, and so on.

## What it tells you

Labor efficiency tells you whether the time you're buying is converting to output. Different problems show up here:

- Low efficiency means you're paying for hours that aren't producing value. Maybe disorganized workflow. Maybe under-scheduled work. Maybe a slow team member. Maybe waiting on materials or instructions.
- A sudden drop usually points to a specific change: a new process that's not working, a new hire who's still ramping, broken equipment, a workflow shift.
- A gradual decline often points to creeping disorganization: shop layout drift, inventory issues, poor scheduling discipline.

Healthy efficiency varies by business. As rough ranges:

- Service-oriented work (where billable hours are the productive ones): 75-90%
- Trade work with travel between jobs: 65-80%
- Production work: 80-95% depending on changeover frequency

## Labor efficiency vs. labor utilization

This trips people up. They're different.

- **Labor utilization** = how full the schedule is. (Hours scheduled or booked ÷ available hours.)
- **Labor efficiency** = how productive the time worked is. (Productive hours ÷ hours actually worked.)

You can be at 95% utilization and 65% efficiency. Calendar full, output low. Different problem, different fix. See [Labor Utilization](/kpis/labor-utilization) for the other side.

## Common mistakes

- **Conflating efficiency with utilization.** They're different. Tracking only one hides half the picture.
- **Not breaking it down by person.** Aggregate efficiency hides individual variance. If two team members are at 90% and two are at 60%, the average looks "okay" but two people need help.
- **Punishing low efficiency without diagnosing.** Low efficiency can be a team problem, a workflow problem, an estimating problem, a materials problem, or a leadership problem. Punish before you diagnose and you'll demoralize a team that's already struggling.
- **Setting unrealistic targets.** Some work is structurally less efficient (lots of travel time, lots of setup). Target what's realistic for your model.

## How to improve it

The biggest levers, in order of typical impact:

**1. Reduce non-productive time.**
Inventory and parts staged before the job starts. Tools in the right place. Travel time minimized. Setup time tightened. Waiting eliminated. Look at where your team's hours are actually going.

**2. Fix scheduling.**
Big efficiency hits come from gaps in the schedule — a team member finishes early and waits 90 minutes for the next job to start. Better scheduling closes those gaps.

**3. Address comebacks and rework.**
Re-doing work is a 100% efficiency drain. Track comeback rate. Train and fix processes that produce them.

**4. Coach individual underperformers.**
If one team member is structurally below the others, training (or replacement) is the lever. Don't tolerate the gap permanently — it drags the whole team.
