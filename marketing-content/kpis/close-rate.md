# Close Rate

## Definition

Close rate is the percentage of estimates, proposals, or quotes that turn into paid jobs. It tells you how effective your team is at converting interest into revenue.

## Formula

```
Close Rate % = (Jobs Won ÷ Total Estimates Sent) × 100
```

Measure over a meaningful window — usually monthly or quarterly. Too short and the number is noisy; too long and you miss recent changes.

## What it tells you

Close rate is one of three numbers that drives revenue (along with lead volume and average ticket). It also acts as a real-time signal on three different things:

- **Your pricing relative to the market.** If your close rate drops suddenly, pricing might be out of sync with what customers expect.
- **Your presentation skill.** If two team members have very different close rates on similar work, the lower one needs coaching.
- **Lead quality.** If close rate is dropping while lead volume is rising, you might be attracting the wrong customers (cheap-buyer marketing, wrong channel mix).

A healthy close rate varies wildly by business type. As a rough guide:

- High-touch service businesses (consulting, contracting, custom work): 25-50%
- Repair / maintenance work: 60-80% (warmer leads, defined needs)
- Cold-outreach sales (new customer prospecting): 5-15%
- Inbound leads from referrals: 50-80%

These are starting points, not laws. Your business has its own healthy range, and tracking the trend matters more than hitting a benchmark.

## Common mistakes

- **Only tracking the wins.** Counting jobs you closed but not estimates you sent gives you no real close rate. You need both numbers.
- **Tracking it inconsistently.** "We close most jobs" isn't a number. Set a process that captures every estimate AND every outcome.
- **Not breaking it down by person, channel, or service line.** Aggregate close rate can hide that your top performer is at 60% and your new hire is at 25%. The diagnosis is in the breakdown.
- **Confusing it with sales pipeline metrics.** Close rate is about the bottom of the funnel (estimate → close). Pipeline metrics are about the top (lead → estimate). Different problems.

## How to improve it

Three places to look:

**1. Pricing.**
If close rate is dropping and you can't tie it to anything else, pricing relative to the market is a likely culprit. Either you've gotten more expensive, or competitors got cheaper, or customers got more price-sensitive. Test the hypothesis before reflexively dropping prices.

**2. Follow-up.**
Most lost estimates aren't actually lost. They're sitting in customer inboxes waiting to be re-engaged. A simple 5-day and 14-day follow-up sequence can lift close rate 10-15 points for businesses that aren't doing it.

**3. Presentation.**
If close rate varies by who's selling, train the underperformers. If close rate is uniformly low, you may need to rethink how options are presented. Are you offering one option (and getting yes-or-no), or three (and getting which-one)? Three options usually closes higher.

## Pairing with average ticket

Close rate and average ticket move in tension. Lower prices usually mean higher close rate but lower ticket. Higher prices mean lower close rate but higher ticket. The sweet spot is the combination that maximizes total revenue, not either number alone.

If you're optimizing for close rate at the expense of ticket, you're probably underpricing. If you're optimizing for ticket at the expense of close rate, you're probably overpricing or under-presenting.
