# Operating Margin

**URL:** `/kpis/operating-margin`
**Meta title:** Operating Margin: Definition, Formula, and What It Tells You | The Good Plans Co
**Meta description:** Operating margin is what's left after both direct costs and overhead. The number that tells you whether your business is actually profitable to run.

---

## Definition

Operating margin is the percentage of revenue that remains after subtracting BOTH direct costs (COGS) and overhead (operating expenses). It tells you whether your business model is actually working, before interest, taxes, and owner draws.

## Formula

```
Operating Margin % = Operating Profit ÷ Revenue × 100

Where Operating Profit = Revenue − COGS − Operating Expenses
```

Operating Expenses includes everything that costs money but isn't tied to a specific job: rent, utilities, software, insurance, office staff salaries, marketing, vehicles, professional fees, owner pay (if you're paying yourself a W-2 salary).

## What it tells you

This is the single most important profitability number in your business. Gross margin tells you whether your pricing and direct costs work. Operating margin tells you whether the whole machine works.

A business can have a 50% gross margin and still lose money if overhead is too heavy. Operating margin catches that.

Healthy ranges vary a lot by business model, but as a rough guide:

- Service businesses with employees: 10-20% is healthy, 20%+ is strong
- Product or trades businesses with inventory + crew: 8-15% is healthy
- Solopreneurs (less applicable here, but for reference): 25-40%+ is common because overhead is minimal

If your operating margin is in the low single digits or negative, the business is structurally underperforming. That's fixable, but it requires looking at both sides of the equation — pricing AND overhead.

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing it with gross margin.** Gross margin = after direct costs. Operating margin = after direct costs AND overhead. They're different conversations.
- **Mixing in interest, taxes, or owner draws.** Operating margin is BEFORE all of those. Including them gives you net margin or something muddier. Keep operating margin clean.
- **Not tracking it month-over-month.** A single month's operating margin can be noisy. The trend over 3-6 months is the meaningful signal.
- **Setting unrealistic targets.** Targeting 30% operating margin in a labor-heavy service business will frustrate you. Target what's realistic for your business model, not what you read about in a SaaS blog.

## How to improve it

Two sides of the equation. Most businesses neglect one of them.

**Side 1: Lift gross margin.**
Pricing review. Material/part markup. Service mix shifts. See [Gross Margin](/kpis/gross-margin) for the levers.

**Side 2: Tighten overhead.**
- Renegotiate rent at renewal
- Review every recurring software subscription (most businesses have 20-30% bloat)
- Right-size your office staff to actual demand
- Audit marketing spend for ROI (most owners are over-spending in the wrong channels)
- Watch vehicle and equipment leasing costs — easy to over-commit

A 2% improvement on each side compounds. A business doing $2M with a 6% operating margin going to 10% is making $80K more profit on the same revenue, no new customers required.
