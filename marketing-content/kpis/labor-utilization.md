# Labor Utilization

**URL:** `/kpis/labor-utilization`
**Meta title:** Labor Utilization: How Full Is Your Schedule, Really? | The Good Plans Co
**Meta description:** Labor utilization is the percentage of available hours that are booked or scheduled. Different from labor efficiency. Here's the difference and what it tells you.

---

## Definition

Labor utilization is the percentage of your team's available work hours that are booked, scheduled, or assigned to revenue-generating activity. It tells you how full the schedule is.

## Formula

```
Labor Utilization % = Hours Booked or Scheduled ÷ Total Available Hours × 100
```

"Available Hours" is everyone's working hours combined. If you have five people working 40 hours each, available is 200 hours. If 170 of those are booked on jobs, utilization is 85%.

## What it tells you

Utilization tells you whether your team has enough work to do. It doesn't tell you whether they're doing it productively (that's [Labor Efficiency](/kpis/labor-efficiency)).

The diagnostic signal:

- **High utilization (85%+)** sustained means you're at or near capacity. Adding more sales without adding more capacity creates wait lists, not revenue.
- **Low utilization (under 60%)** sustained means you have a sales problem. You're paying for capacity you're not using.
- **Volatile utilization** swinging between extremes usually means a scheduling or sales-cycle problem.

Healthy sustained utilization is usually 75-85%. Above that and burnout risk creeps in, plus you have no buffer for surprises. Below that and you're paying for capacity that's not producing.

## The common confusion

Owners often look at utilization and feel good about a high number. "We're at 95% utilization, we're crushing it."

But high utilization with low efficiency means a full calendar producing very little. The two metrics tell different stories and need to be tracked separately:

- **Utilization high, efficiency high:** healthy, scaling well
- **Utilization high, efficiency low:** "busy but broke" — the most painful operational state
- **Utilization low, efficiency high:** sales problem, not an ops problem
- **Utilization low, efficiency low:** rare, usually a leadership or systemic issue

The fixes are completely different in each case. See [Labor Efficiency](/kpis/labor-efficiency) for the other half.

## Common mistakes

- **Tracking utilization but not efficiency.** Tells you half the story. You can be 95% utilized and losing money.
- **Setting 100% utilization as the goal.** 100% sustained utilization means no buffer for surprises, no time for training or process improvement, high burnout risk. Some buffer is healthy.
- **Confusing utilization with billable hours.** Billable is the OUTCOME of utilization × efficiency. Don't conflate them in your tracking.
- **Aggregating across the team.** Individual utilization variance can hide that one person is at 60% and another is at 95%. Often pointed at by team morale issues.

## How to improve it

If utilization is low, the lever is usually NOT operations:

**1. Increase lead volume.**
More leads → more estimates → more booked work. Marketing, referrals, networking, outbound — the top of the funnel.

**2. Improve close rate.**
Same lead volume, more closed work. See [Close Rate](/kpis/close-rate).

**3. Reduce sales cycle length.**
If estimates sit for 6 weeks before closing, your scheduling is reactive. Tightening the close-to-start window evens out utilization.

If utilization is too high (sustained 90%+):

- Hire (carefully — make sure you're not just hiring to mask an efficiency problem)
- Raise prices (use the demand to filter to higher-value work)
- Add a service line or shift mix toward higher-margin work
