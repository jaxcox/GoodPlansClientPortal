## The instinct that doesn't work

When a KPI is underperforming, the instinct is to focus on it. Revenue is down? "Let's drive revenue." Margin is slipping? "Let's protect margin." Close rate is dropping? "Let's close more."

That instinct is almost always wrong.

Output KPIs (revenue, margin, profit) are the SCORE of the game. You can't change the score by staring harder at the scoreboard. You change the score by changing the things that produce the score. Those things are usually OTHER KPIs — the inputs.

This is the foundation of the diagnostic work we do every couple of weeks with clients. When a number is off, you don't fix the number. You trace upstream and fix whatever's actually broken.

## KPIs as a tree

Think of your KPIs as a tree.

At the top: your big output numbers. Revenue. Gross profit. Operating profit. These are what you want to move, but you can't move them directly.

Underneath: the operational KPIs that feed the outputs. Things like average ticket, close rate, lead volume, capacity utilization, labor efficiency, customer count, retention rate.

At the bottom: the inputs you actually control. Estimates written. Calls answered. Quotes followed up on. Marketing campaigns. Hiring decisions. Pricing changes.

When revenue is down, you don't push on revenue. You walk down the tree. Is lead volume down? Is close rate down? Is average ticket down? Once you know which branch is the problem, you keep walking. Why is close rate down? Are estimates not being followed up? Is the pricing wrong for the market? Is the service advisor new?

Eventually you hit something at the bottom of the tree — a specific action or input — that you can actually move. That's the lever. Everything upstream of it will follow.

## A specific example: revenue is down

You look at last quarter's numbers. Revenue is 11% behind target. The instinct is "we need more revenue." That's the wrong question.

The right question: where in the tree did revenue lose 11%?

Walk down:

- **Was it volume?** Number of jobs completed is roughly flat with last year. Volume isn't the issue.
- **Was it average ticket?** Average ticket is down 8% vs. last year. There's a lot of the 11% right there.
- **Why is average ticket down?** We've been booking smaller jobs. Our larger-job close rate dropped from 38% to 22% over the last six months.
- **Why is our larger-job close rate down?** Two reasons we can find: a new local competitor at lower price points, and a service advisor who joined six months ago and isn't presenting larger options with confidence.
- **What can we actually do?** Train the new advisor. Re-evaluate pricing on the larger jobs to see if we need a different value framing.

Now we have a specific lever to pull. The revenue problem becomes a confidence-and-presentation problem, which becomes a training-and-pricing problem.

Compare that to the "let's just drive more revenue" version. That one would have us pushing harder on marketing, sales calls, and maybe ad spend — all of which would be wasted effort because the leak is at the bottom of the funnel, not the top.
